4. Conviction scores and the model book

Conviction scores are the numerical output of a documented process. A score is not a rating, a price target, a forecast of return, or a buy, sell, or hold instruction. It summarizes the system's assessment of a thesis against evidence available on the date shown, under the methodology published on this site. Scores change when the evidence changes, and the history of each score is preserved and visible.

The model book is a hypothetical portfolio. No money is invested in it. Its positions, entry prices, weights, and any figures derived from them are hypothetical. Hypothetical results have inherent limitations. They do not reflect actual trading, transaction costs, taxes, market impact, liquidity, or the difficulty of executing at the recorded prices, and no representation is made that any account has achieved or will achieve results similar to those shown. Entry prices are read from public sources on the entry date and may differ from prices available to any reader. Position weights are expressed as a percentage of a notional amount at cost, not at market value, unless stated otherwise.

The model book currently publishes no performance figures. If a performance series is published in the future, it will begin on a stated inception date, will be computed by a stated method, will be presented with this disclosure, and will not be restated to include periods before its inception. Past and hypothetical performance are not indicative of future results.

7. How the research is produced, and its limits

The research on this site is produced by an automated pipeline that gathers primary-source documents (including filings with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission and foreign regulators), market and financial data from third-party providers, and published commentary by third parties; extracts figures from those sources; and drafts analysis with the assistance of large language models. The publisher reviews outputs before publication and governs the system through written rules that are themselves published.

Every figure on this site carries a tag identifying its source class: a filing, a company release, market data, a calculation from tagged inputs, a third-party author, or an inference made by the system. Inferences are labeled as inferences and are published with a stated condition under which they would be considered wrong.

Despite these controls, the content may contain errors. Source documents may be misread, third-party data may be wrong or stale, extraction may fail, and language models can produce plausible text that is incorrect. Nothing on this site is warranted to be accurate, complete, or current. Readers should verify any figure against the primary source before relying on it.

8. Third-party authors, public figures, and graded records

This site records dated public statements made by third-party authors and public figures about companies, technologies, and markets, and grades those statements against information that later becomes public, principally company filings. The grades reflect this site's published methodology applied to specific, identified statements. A grade is the publisher's opinion about a specific statement on a specific date. It is not a characterization of any person's ability, integrity, or overall record, and no such characterization should be inferred.

Third-party authors and public figures named on this site have no affiliation with this site and have not endorsed it. This site links to their own publications rather than reproducing them. Quotations are brief and are used for the purpose of identifying and evaluating the statement being graded. Any person who believes a statement has been attributed to them inaccurately, or graded on a misreading, may contact the publisher at contact@pschafer.com; corrections are made under the policy in §10.

11. Risks specific to this coverage

The securities discussed on this site are concentrated in a single theme: the physical infrastructure of artificial intelligence. Their prices are highly sensitive to the pace of capital spending by a small number of large technology companies, to semiconductor and energy cycles, to export controls and trade policy, and to technological substitution that can relieve a supply constraint quickly. Several are small- or mid-capitalization companies with limited trading liquidity, high volatility, and, in some cases, no current profits. Some are foreign issuers whose filings are made in other languages under other regulatory regimes. A reader who acts on any information here could lose some or all of the money involved.
